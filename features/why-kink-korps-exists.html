Why Kink Korps Exists

Freedom and accountability belong in the same room.

By Satanica Lux · September 2026

Mr. Satan created Kink Korps because South Florida didn’t need another party calling itself judgment-free while quietly deciding which forms of kink were acceptable.

The name “Korps” reflects what he had in mind from the beginning. Like the Marine Corps, he pictured a close-knit group whose members stand together and look out for one another. I helped turn his original idea into an event company centered around something we both felt was missing from the local scene.

We wanted a space where people with more extreme interests weren’t merely tolerated. We wanted them to feel welcome expressing themselves openly, as long as everything taking place was consensual, legal, and safe.

Kink Spaces Still Kink-Shame

Mr. Satan and I have both been chastised for being exhibitionists. We’ve attended parties where consensual and legal activities were prohibited for no reason beyond the organizers feeling uncomfortable with them.

And exhibitionism isn’t the only thing people judge.

Full nudity, gay play, trans guests, and activities viewed as “too extreme” still draw disapproving looks from people who describe themselves as old school.

There’s something almost absurd about entering a community built around sexual freedom only to find another set of rules about the “right” way to be unconventional.

Kink Korps exists in opposition to that mindset.

Your version of kink doesn’t need anyone else’s approval.

Be loud. Be quiet. Show up naked or fully dressed. Be experienced or brand new. Be gay, straight, trans, cisgender, curious, exhibitionistic, private, actively playing, or happy watching from across the room.

You don’t need to prove your sexuality or your kink is respectable enough to belong here.

Judgment-Free Doesn’t Mean Consequence-Free

“Anything goes” doesn’t mean consent disappears.

Too many men still hear a boundary and treat it like the start of a negotiation. They push. They argue. They hover. They keep asking after someone has already said no.

Solo women shouldn’t spend an entire night trying to escape groups of horny men who refuse to accept an answer.

When someone crosses a nonviolent boundary, our first response is a direct conversation. We speak to adults like adults. We explain what happened, why the behavior was a problem, and what needs to change.

If the same behavior continues, the person isn’t invited back.

Serious violations are different. Nonconsensual touching, threats, assault, or similar behavior means immediate removal.

There’s a major difference between kink-shaming someone and holding them accountable for disrespecting another person’s boundaries.

No event organizer gets to promise nobody will ever behave badly. What we promise is simpler. When someone raises a concern, we take it seriously. When someone’s boundaries or safety are threatened, we respond.

Freedom only works when everyone in the room respects everyone else’s right to say no.

The Korps Is Everyone in the Room

Kink Korps isn’t only Mr. Satan and me.

Everyone who walks through the door becomes part of what makes the space work. The whole idea depends on community. We protect each other’s freedom to express ourselves, and we look out for each other while doing it.

Behind the scenes, Mr. Satan and I naturally fell into separate roles.

I handle administration and social media. Mr. Satan handles the music and serves as what I affectionately call the “people wrangler.”

No, it’s not an official title.

He knows everyone. Everyone gravitates toward him. Once guests walk through the door, I consider them his responsibility.

Somehow, it works.

We bring different personalities and different strengths to the same purpose.

When We Knew It Worked

Anything Goes Party is Kink Korps’ recurring event. Every edition has its own theme, but the promise behind each one stays the same.

Anything fucking goes.

Within consent, safety, and the law, guests decide what those words mean for themselves.

I knew we had created something special during our first party while walking to the restroom.

I looked around the venue and noticed people weren’t standing awkwardly against the walls waiting for someone else to make the first move.

They were having fun.

They were playing.

They were using the space exactly as we had hoped they would.

People felt comfortable enough to be themselves without constantly looking over their shoulders and wondering whether someone else approved.

For me, that was success.

A kink party shouldn’t feel like a regular nightclub with a dress code and a few pieces of dungeon furniture scattered around. People shouldn’t fill a room only to feel too nervous, restricted, or judged to participate.

We wanted people to feel free.

That night, they did.

This Is Only the Beginning

Kink Korps was never meant to stop with one recurring party.

We’re building more events, custom kink furniture, clothing, and other projects around the same community.

The form will change. The purpose won’t.

Expression without kink-shaming.

Freedom built around consent.

A community where people look out for their own.

Follow Kink Korps. Come to an Anything Goes Party.

If you’ve spent years hearing you’re too extreme, there’s room for you here.

If you’re still figuring out what freedom and self-expression look like for you, there’s room for you too.

Welcome to the Korps.
