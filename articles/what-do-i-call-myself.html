What Do I Call Myself?

You can choose a label, choose a scene name, choose both, or choose neither.

By Satanica Lux · September 2026

One of the first questions people run into when entering the scene is what to call themselves.

Are you a top? A bottom? A Dom? A submissive? A switch? A sadist? A masochist? A brat? A voyeur? Something more specific? Nothing at all?

Then comes the second question: are you using your real name?

You do not need answers before you walk through the door.

Labels Are Tools, Not Assignments

A useful label can give you language for something you already recognize in yourself. It can help you find education, identify people with similar interests, and explain what you might want to explore.

It is not an application you submit to the kink authorities. Nobody stamps it permanent.

Use the language that helps. Leave the rest alone. “Curious” is a label if you want it to be. “I don’t know yet” works beautifully.

Top and Bottom Are Not Dom and Sub

A top generally performs an action. A bottom receives it. Dominant and submissive describe a power dynamic. Those things sometimes travel together, but they are not interchangeable.

A person can bottom without being submissive. A submissive can direct the practical details of a scene. A switch may enjoy different roles with different people—or on different nights. Context matters more than the word in a profile.

A Label Is Not Consent

Calling yourself a submissive does not mean every dominant can command you. Calling yourself a masochist does not give strangers permission to hurt you. “Brat” is not consent to punishment. “Exhibitionist” is not consent to photographs.

A label can begin a conversation. It cannot finish the negotiation.

People still need to ask what the word means to you, what you want with them, and what is off the table tonight.

Why People Use Scene Names

A lot of us use scene names. Sometimes the reason is privacy. We have jobs, families, children, clients, neighbors, or complicated lives that do not need to become searchable because somebody tagged a party photo.

Sometimes the name is creative. It belongs to a version of us that feels more honest after dark. Sometimes it started as a username and followed us into real rooms. Sometimes it just sounds better shouted across a crowded club.

You can introduce yourself with a scene name and leave it there. Nobody is entitled to your legal name as proof that you trust them.

Choose Something You Can Live In

Your scene name can be dramatic, elegant, threatening, ridiculous, ordinary, or completely unrelated to kink. It should be something you can hear without cringing and say without delivering a backstory every time.

Before you commit, say it aloud. Imagine putting it on a ticket. Search it to see whether a well-known local person already uses it. Think about whether it reveals more than you intend—your birth year, employer, neighborhood, or the username attached to the rest of your life.

You are also allowed to test a name and replace it later. This is a community, not the Social Security Administration.

A Name Is Not Automatically a Title

Some people use titles such as Sir, Ma’am, Mistress, Master, Daddy, Mommy, or Goddess within specific dynamics. Those titles may carry personal meaning and are not automatically owed by everyone in the room.

Use the name a person gives you. Ask before adding a title. Respecting somebody’s identity does not require pretending you are in a relationship or power exchange that you never agreed to enter.

Respect the Name You Were Given

Do not interrogate somebody about whether a scene name is “real.” Do not announce a legal name you happen to know. Do not connect their kink account to their civilian identity, tag them without permission, or play detective because you are curious.

Privacy is not dishonesty. It is a boundary.

Leave Room to Become Someone Else

The label that helps you today may feel too narrow next year. The name you chose at your first party may stop sounding like you. Growth does not mean you were lying before.

Name yourself if it gives you freedom. Label yourself if it gives you language. Change either one when it stops serving you.

And if nothing fits yet, you can simply say your name—whatever name you chose—and let people meet the person before the category.
