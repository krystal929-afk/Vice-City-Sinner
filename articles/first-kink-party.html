The Beginner’s Guide to Not Being That Person

Nobody expects you to know everything. They do expect you to act like you have sense.

By Satanica Lux · September 2026

Being new is allowed. Acting like other people are props in your personal sexual adventure is not.

You do not need to know every term, own expensive gear, or walk into your first party with a catalog of things you are ready to do. You can show up, learn the rules, buy a drink, watch the room, have a conversation, dance, and go home. That counts as participating.

What people will notice is whether you treat them like people.

House Rules Are Not Suggestions

Every event has its own rules about photography, nudity, play areas, alcohol, smoking, dress, and what happens when somebody has a problem. Listen to the orientation. Read what you were given. If you are unsure, ask a host before you improvise.

“Another party lets me do it” is not an argument. You are at this party.

Ask Before You Touch Anything

That includes bodies, clothes, hair, collars, toys, rope, furniture currently being used, and the beautiful piece of equipment you suddenly feel qualified to investigate.

Compliments are not permission. Flirting is not permission. Someone being naked in the same building as you is definitely not permission.

People are not interactive exhibits just because you entered a kink space.

Watching Has Etiquette

It is usually fine to watch public play from a respectful distance. It is not fine to crowd the people involved, interrupt their scene, offer commentary, touch their equipment, or position yourself so close that you become an uninvited participant.

If you cannot tell whether you are too close, move back.

No Is the End of the Question

A no is not an opening offer. Do not ask why. Do not immediately propose a smaller version. Do not hover nearby hoping the answer changes. Do not make someone comfort you because you were rejected.

Say, “Okay,” and keep being normal.

The same applies when somebody changes their mind. Consent can be withdrawn in the middle of a conversation, a kiss, a scene, or anything else. What happened five minutes ago does not create an obligation now.

You Do Not Have to Perform

New people sometimes think they need to prove they belong by playing immediately, wearing less than they want, choosing a label, or agreeing to something intense. You do not.

“I’m only watching tonight” is a complete plan. So is “I’m open to conversation, not play.” So is leaving early because the room is not for you.

A useful introduction: “I’m new and mostly observing tonight, but I’d love to hear what you enjoy about the community.” Honest, friendly, and no promises attached.

If Something Feels Wrong, Tell Someone

You do not need to conduct an investigation before speaking to a host. If somebody keeps pushing, touches you without consent, threatens you, or makes you feel unsafe, bring it to the event staff. A responsible organizer would rather hear a concern than discover later that everyone assumed somebody else would handle it.

You are not expected to know everything on your first night. Pay attention. Ask questions. Accept answers. Respect the room, and give yourself permission to move at your own speed.

That is enough.
