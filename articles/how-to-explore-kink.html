CURIOUS DOESN'T MEAN COMMITTED.

How to explore an interest without rushing yourself, performing for anyone, or promising more than you mean.

BY THE VICE CITY SINNER · AUGUST 2026

1. NOTICE

Begin with what catches your attention—not what you think a kinky person is supposed to want. A reaction is information, not an assignment.

2. NAME

Ask yourself whether this is a fantasy you enjoy privately, something you want to learn about, something you would like to watch, or something you want to try. Those categories can overlap, but they do not have to.

3. RESEARCH

Look for education that explains risks, anatomy, technique, communication, and emergency response—not only the glamorous final image. For rope, impact, breath-related activities, bondage, electrical play, substances, and anything else with meaningful physical risk, seek instruction from qualified practitioners.

4. NEGOTIATE

Before play, discuss the specific act, hard and soft limits, signals or safewords, relevant injuries and health concerns, safer-sex choices, privacy, sobriety, stopping, and aftercare. “We’ll figure it out” is not a complete plan.

Consent should be informed, specific, voluntary, reversible, and possible to communicate.

5. TRY SMALL

Choose the lowest-stakes version first. Watch a demonstration. Hold the rope. Try a command. Practice a position while fully clothed. Set a short time limit. Keep an easy exit.

6. CHECK IN

Afterward, ask what felt good, strange, disappointing, activating, funny, or unexpectedly tender. Emotional responses do not always arrive on schedule. Adjust without grading yourself or your partner.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING YOURSELF

  • What part of this interests me: sensation, service, control, ritual, aesthetics, trust, fear, praise, humiliation, restraint, performance, or something else?
  • Would I still want it if nobody else knew?
  • What would make this feel safer?
  • What would make me stop immediately?
  • What do I want afterward?
Remember: You can enjoy an idea without adopting a label. You can change labels. You can leave a community, dynamic, or practice that no longer fits. Nobody gets permanent rights to a version of you.
