You Are Not Too Much

Some rooms only know how to make people smaller.

By Satanica Lux · September 2026

There are a hundred ways people can tell you that you’re too much without ever using those words.

Too loud, too sexual, too emotional, too intense, too honest, too strange, too visible. The instructions arrive just as quickly: calm down, cover up, lower your voice, stop asking questions, and do not make anybody uncomfortable.

Hear it often enough and eventually you start doing the work for them.

You edit yourself before anyone asks. You apologize before you’ve done anything. You explain every desire until it sounds harmless enough for somebody else. You make yourself easier to understand, easier to manage, easier to ignore.

People may like that version of you better.

That doesn’t mean they know you.

The kink community is supposed to be one of the places where we can stop doing this. Sometimes it is. Sometimes it just hands us another fucking rulebook.

You can be kinky, naked, queer, or sexual, but only within the narrow version that keeps everybody watching comfortable. Freedom is welcomed right up until it becomes inconvenient.

And before anyone decides that “be yourself” means “do whatever the fuck you want”: it doesn’t.

If someone says no, stop. If you hurt someone, own it. If you cross a boundary, consequences are not kink-shaming. Freedom without accountability is just another person forcing everyone else to carry the cost of their behavior.

Accountability asks you to change what you did. Shame asks you to apologize for what you are.

Learn the difference.

This has been hitting home for me lately for reasons that have nothing to do with kink. I don’t need to put all of those reasons on the page for the truth of it to matter.

Sometimes you look at everything that isn’t working and wonder whether you’re the problem. Maybe if you were quieter, easier, less emotional, less demanding, less honest, things wouldn’t hurt so much.

Sometimes you do need to apologize. Sometimes you need to do better.

And sometimes you need to leave the room that keeps asking you to disappear.

Not every person, relationship, or community can hold the full version of you. Their limits may be real, but they are not automatically a verdict on your worth.

You can respect someone else’s boundaries without treating your own existence like a violation.

That is what I want The Vice City Sinner to be.

Not a place without standards. A place with the right ones: consent, honesty, respect, accountability, and room for people who have spent too much of their lives being edited into something more acceptable.

If this first issue leaves you with one thing, let it be this:

You can take responsibility without taking yourself apart, and you can respect a no without treating your own existence like a violation. You can lose a person, a place, or a version of your life without accepting the story that you were too much for wanting to live it fully.

Sometimes the room was simply too small. Leave with all of yourself.

We’ll make room.
