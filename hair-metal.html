I’m Bringing Back Hair Metal Fashion. Sorry Not Sorry.

Keep the black. Turn everything else up.

By Satanica Lux · The Vice City Sinner
Jump to the outfit guide ↓

I wear black almost every day.

Black shirt. Black skirt. Black boots. Black tights. Black jacket. My nails are usually black or red. If there is actual color involved, it is probably in my hair, my earrings, or some piece of jewelry I decided needed to be obnoxious enough to carry the entire outfit.

I have no intention of changing that.

What I am interested in changing is the moment when an all-black alternative wardrobe starts becoming less of a personal style and more of a uniform you assembled on autopilot.

You know the one. Same boots. Same black top. Same eyeliner. Same jewelry. Technically nothing is wrong with it, but nothing is particularly happening either.

That is where hair metal comes crashing through the wall.

I am officially campaigning for a hair metal fashion revival, but before anybody reaches for bleach, a teasing comb and enough hairspray to violate several environmental treaties, that is not what I mean.

I do not want to recreate 1987.

I want to steal from it.

Hair metal understood something that alternative fashion occasionally forgets: getting dressed is allowed to be fucking fun.

The original glam-metal look had absolutely no interest in restraint. Leather, chains, mesh, spandex, makeup, jewelry and hair engineered to have its own gravitational field were all fair game. Bands like Poison pushed the glam element so far that even other metal bands made fun of them for it, which honestly only strengthens my case.

The point was never tasteful moderation.

The point was being seen.

The point was having the freedom to express yourself as loudly, strangely or joyfully as you wanted.

That is the part worth bringing back.

The Quick Guide

Nine No-Brainer Hair Metal Revival Combos

For waking up an all-black wardrobe without buying an entirely new personality.

1

The Satanica Default

Black fitted top + black mini + fishnets or torn tights + platform creepers + stacked silver jewelry

2

Band Tee, But Make It Hot

Band tee + leather mini or shredded black jeans + studded belt + layered necklaces + combat boots

3

Leather Does the Heavy Lifting

Simple black base + moto jacket + chains + smoky eye + platform boots

4

One Violent Color

All black + one electric accent

5

Hair Is the Outfit

Simple black clothes + fashion-color hair + braids, faux-hawk, or metal hair accessories

6

Lace and Damage

Black lace or mesh + leather or denim + chains + combat boots

7

Maximum Jewelry, Minimum Effort

Black tank + black jeans or skirt + rings + cuffs + layered necklaces + statement earrings

8

Five-Minute Full Volume

Black dress + leather jacket + platform boots + giant earrings + lipstick

9

Feeling Kinky?

Statement jacket + pasties + micro mini + fishnets + thigh-high boots

Masc option: swap the mini for fitted black jeans or leather-look leggings.

The rule: Start with what you already wear. Add one source of drama, one source of texture, and one thing that feels a little excessive.

Keep the Black. Turn Everything Else Up.

The easiest misconception to make here is assuming that a hair metal influence requires a colorful wardrobe.

Absolutely not.

My wardrobe remains black.

What hair metal gives me is permission to make that black wardrobe louder through texture, silhouette, hardware, jewelry, hair and makeup.

A plain black tank with plain black jeans is just a black tank with black jeans. Put it under battered leather, stack several bracelets up both wrists, add chains, oversized earrings and boots that could reasonably injure somebody, and now we have a situation.

You do not necessarily need more clothes.

You might just need more styling.

That distinction is especially useful when you have built an alternative wardrobe over years and suddenly feel bored with it. Before buying an entirely new personality, start looking at the clothes you already own as a base layer.

The outfit does not always need another garment.

Sometimes it needs another necklace.

And another one.

Jewelry Should Probably Be Doing Too Much

Minimalist jewelry has its place.

That place is occasionally somewhere far away from me.

Hair metal gives us an excellent excuse to bring back stacked bracelets, layered chains, charms, cuffs, rings, chokers, oversized earrings and whatever weird metal object you found that technically qualifies as wearable.

Conveniently, maximalist jewelry is having a broader fashion moment again. Fall 2026 runways have leaned heavily into oversized statement earrings, layered necklaces, charms and deliberately abundant accessorizing.

This is one place where I will absolutely use color.

If the entire outfit is black, electric-blue earrings suddenly look incredible. So do neon acrylic pieces, red stones, weird resin earrings, chrome, silver, spikes, beads, skulls or something ridiculous you bought from an artist at a market because it made you laugh.

Your accessories do not have to whisper simply because your clothes are monochrome.

We Need Dramatic Hair Again

This is where I am going to respectfully leave traditional hair-metal accuracy in the past.

You do not need teased blonde hair.

I certainly do not need teased blonde hair. Blonde and I have met. We have agreed to remain acquaintances.

The modern version of big rock-and-roll hair is not necessarily about literal size anyway. It is about making the hair part of the outfit again.

Fashion colors. Intricate braids. Braided faux-hawks. Undercuts. Shaved sides. Extensions. Sculpted ponytails. Hair cuffs. Chains worked through braids. Rings, clips, spikes, contrasting sections of color, dramatic texture.

Braids themselves are already moving back toward more elaborate and expressive forms in 2026, including updated crown braids and softer, more creative braided styles.

That gives us an enormous playground.

Electric-blue hair with an otherwise completely black outfit already does more visually than five extra accessories could. Add side braids with metal cuffs or pull it into a faux-hawk and suddenly the same clothes you wore last week look completely different.

That is the revival I am interested in.

Not copying somebody else’s decade.

Taking its nerve.

Sexy Does Not Have to Mean Delicate

Hair metal was also wonderfully uninterested in keeping masculine and feminine styling in separate little boxes.

Men wore eyeliner, lipstick, jewelry, skin-tight pants and hair that required maintenance most women would consider unreasonable. Women borrowed leather, studs, boots and the harder edges of rock styling right back.

That tension still works.

A leather jacket over a tiny fitted top.

A shredded band shirt with a mini skirt.

A bodycon black dress with enormous boots and enough metal around your neck to trip airport security.

Lace next to leather. Fishnet next to denim. A severe collar with giant colorful earrings.

You can be feminine without looking fragile. You can dress sexy without dressing soft. You can wear something brutally heavy and still put on lipstick because you fucking felt like it.

Those things have never been contradictions.

Style Is Not a Membership Exam

The other reason I want to borrow from hair metal is that alternative fashion has become very good at categorizing itself.

Goth goes here. Punk goes there. Metal over there. Fetishwear in this particular drawer. Y2K gets its own Pinterest board. Someone somewhere will inevitably inform you that the boots you bought are technically associated with the wrong microgenre.

I am begging everyone to relax.

You are allowed to take things.

Not sacred cultural items. Not somebody else’s identity. I mean stylistically.

Take the giant earrings.

Take the chains.

Take a leather silhouette from one scene, makeup from another decade, a metal shirt you have owned for fifteen years and some ridiculous boots you found online.

Personal style gets interesting when you stop treating it like a test you can fail or assuming you need permission to begin.

Maybe you spent years dressing to satisfy somebody else. Maybe you were discouraged from wearing what you loved, controlled by a partner or simply never had the time and safety to explore. If you finally get the chance to express yourself after decades of being told who to be, you do not owe anyone a gradual, historically accurate introduction. You are allowed to start wherever you are and go as big as you want.

Hair metal was excessive because excess was part of the pleasure. That is the actual lesson worth resurrecting.

Your version does not have to look anything like mine.

Maybe you love color. Use it.

Maybe you hate skirts. Fine.

Maybe you are masculine, feminine, neither, both or whatever the hell you felt like being when you woke up.

The formula is not specific garments. It is more personality per square inch.

So yes, I am bringing hair metal fashion back.

Not the costume. Not the carbon copy. Not because I secretly want everyone walking around looking like Poison.

I want the leather, ornamentation, drama, sexual confidence and complete lack of shame about being visually excessive.

Keep whatever already makes your style yours.

Then turn the fucking volume up.
